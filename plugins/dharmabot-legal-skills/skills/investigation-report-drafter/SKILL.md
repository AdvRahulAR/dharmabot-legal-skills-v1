# Investigation Report Drafter

Every output is a draft for supervising counsel to check before it is issued.

## Purpose

Write a report that a board, auditor, regulator or court could rely on: facts separated from inferences, every finding traced to evidence, and the subject's response stated fairly.

## Jurisdiction gate

Confirm the investigation concerns an Indian entity or conduct governed by Indian law. Record the company type, regulators and the body receiving the report. If the report may go to a foreign regulator or parent, flag privilege and data-transfer questions for counsel qualified in that jurisdiction.

## Required inputs

**Blocking**: the terms of reference; the allegations; the evidence (documents, interview notes, data analysis) with an index; the subject's response or a record that one was sought; and the standard of proof the instructing body set.

Also useful: the investigation log, policies breached, and prior findings on similar issues.

## Method

1. Retrieve the current official text of the policies, codes and laws against which conduct is assessed (for example the company's code of conduct, Companies Act duties, the BNS offences if referral is considered, DPDP Act limits on what personal data the report may contain). Record source and date.
2. Decide the privilege strategy with the instructing counsel: who the addressee is, marking, and circulation. Keep factual annexures separate where it helps.
3. Structure: executive summary; background and terms of reference; scope and limitations; method; allegations one by one; findings; subject's response; conclusions; recommendations.
4. For each allegation: the relevant evidence with exhibit references; what is established; what is inferred and why; what is not established; the subject's explanation and the assessment of it.
5. State findings on the agreed standard (for example balance of probabilities). Never state that a crime was committed; say whether material may warrant referral.
6. Record limitations: unavailable witnesses, missing data, time limits, and documents the subject was not shown.
7. Recommendations: disciplinary action (via `disciplinary-inquiry-drafter`), control fixes, recovery, insurer notice, regulator or police referral, and protection of the whistleblower.
8. Draft audience summaries: board, audit committee and auditor versions, each shorter and limited to what that audience needs.

## Output

1. The full report with an exhibit index.
2. Audience summaries.
3. A circulation and privilege note.

## Guardrails and hand-offs

- Never present inference as fact or omit the subject's response.
- Never invent evidence, quotes or amounts.
- Minimise personal data; name the whistleblower only if strictly necessary and authorised.
- For gaps that need more work, go back to `internal-investigation-planner`.
- Before anything is filed, sent or relied on, run `pre-filing-verification-checker` to verify every authority and quotation, and keep its log with the file.
