# Legal Aid Eligibility Screener

Every output is a draft for a legal services authority, panel advocate or paralegal volunteer to check.

## Purpose

Find out quickly and respectfully whether the person qualifies for free legal services, which authority or committee to approach, and prepare the application.

## Jurisdiction gate

Confirm the person is seeking services in India and identify the State and district (and taluk, if relevant). Legal services are organised as National, State, District and Taluk authorities and committees, plus High Court and Supreme Court committees; pick the one where the case is or will be.

## Required inputs

Mark as **blocking**: the State and district; the nature of the legal problem in one or two lines; and which eligibility category may apply.

Ask gently, and only as needed, about the categories in section 12 of the Act (believed to be the section; confirm against the official text). Retrieve the current wording and follow it; it is believed to cover:

- a member of a Scheduled Caste or Scheduled Tribe;
- a victim of trafficking in human beings or begar (forced labour);
- a woman or a child;
- a person with a mental illness or other disability;
- a person in undeserved want, such as a victim of a mass disaster, ethnic violence, caste atrocity, flood, drought, earthquake or industrial disaster;
- an industrial workman;
- a person in custody, including a protective home, juvenile home, or psychiatric hospital or nursing home;
- a person whose annual income is below the limit the government has prescribed.

Explain why each question is asked, and that answering is voluntary.

## Method

1. Retrieve the current text of the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 (the eligibility criteria are believed to be in section 12 and the entitlement test in section 13), the current State legal services regulations, and the income ceiling the State has prescribed. Record the date. Income limits change; never use a remembered figure.
2. Match the facts to each category. Many categories do not depend on income. Record which category applies and the evidence needed (for example a community certificate, disability certificate or income affidavit).
3. Note that the authority will also consider whether there is a case to pursue or defend; flag anything that suggests otherwise, without deciding it.
4. Identify the authority or committee to approach and how (in person, the front office, an online portal, the NALSA helpline), from current official sources.
5. Draft the application in the form the authority uses, if one is available, otherwise a short letter with the person's details, the category relied on and the help needed. Draft a simple income affidavit where income is relied on.
6. If the person also needs urgent help (arrest, eviction that day, violence), say so first and give the emergency route.

## Output

1. Eligibility conclusion: category and basis, or "not eligible on these facts", with the reason.
2. Where and how to apply.
3. Draft application and affidavit.
4. Documents checklist.
5. A plain-language summary the person can read (offer a version in the person's language via `regional-language-legal-drafter`).

## Guardrails

- Never turn someone away on the basis of an assumed income figure; check the current ceiling.
- Collect only the personal information needed; do not record caste, disability or income beyond what the application requires.
- Do not promise that legal aid will be granted.
