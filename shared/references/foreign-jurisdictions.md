# Foreign jurisdictions: India's treaty position and official sources

Compiled 14 October 2026. **A starting point, not authority.** Treaty status and notifications change; confirm each item from the official source (Gazette of India, HCCH status tables, the foreign government's site) before relying on it.

Other reciprocating territories under s.44A are believed to include Bangladesh, Malaysia, Trinidad and Tobago, New Zealand, the Cook Islands (including Niue), Western Samoa, Hong Kong, Papua New Guinea, Fiji and Aden. Confirm the current list from the Gazette notifications.

India's New York Convention accession carries both the reciprocity and commercial reservations. India's Hague Service Convention declarations include a Central Authority in the Ministry of Law and Justice and an objection to service through postal and other Article 10 channels; confirm the current declarations on the HCCH status table.

## Courts and legal systems

- **United States:** federal courts and 50 State systems; most commercial law is State law. Identify the State and whether the matter is federal or State.
- **United Kingdom:** England and Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland are separate legal systems. "UK law" usually means England and Wales; confirm.
- **United Arab Emirates:** onshore federal courts and local court systems (Dubai Courts, Abu Dhabi Judicial Department, Ras Al Khaimah Judicial Department), working in Arabic under civil law. The DIFC Courts and ADGM Courts are common-law courts sitting in English, within their own financial free zones.
- **European Union:** EU law (Treaties, Regulations, Directives, CJEU case law) sits alongside each member State's national law and courts. A Directive needs the member State's implementing law.
- **Singapore:** common law; Supreme Court (High Court and Court of Appeal) and the Singapore International Commercial Court.

## Professional rules that matter for Indian lawyers doing foreign work

- **Foreign lawyers in India:** the Bar Council of India Rules for Registration and Regulation of Foreign Lawyers and Foreign Law Firms in India (2022, notified March 2023, amended May 2025) allow registered foreign lawyers to practise foreign and international law and international commercial arbitration, in non-litigious matters only, with a limited fly-in fly-out allowance. Confirm the current Rules.
- **Indian lawyers on US matters:** ABA Formal Opinion 08-451 (2008) allows US lawyers to outsource legal work, including abroad, if they supervise it (Model Rules 5.1 and 5.3), protect confidentiality (Rule 1.6), usually get the client's informed consent, and charge reasonably. Rule 5.5 bars assisting unauthorised practice. The Indian lawyer works under the US lawyer's supervision and does not advise the US client directly on US law.
- **UK:** under the SRA Code for Firms, a firm that outsources work keeps regulatory responsibility for it. Expect equivalent oversight and confidentiality terms.
- **Receiving foreign fees:** fees come in through an authorised dealer bank with an inward-remittance advice. Legal services to a recipient outside India, paid in convertible foreign exchange, can qualify as an export of services for GST, which is zero-rated; file a Letter of Undertaking to export without paying IGST. Confirm current RBI purpose codes, realisation rules and GST conditions.

## Data protection

- **India:** DPDP Act, 2023, section 16 allows transfers abroad except to countries the government restricts by notification (none notified as at the last check). The DPDP Rules, 2025 were published in the Gazette on 13 November 2025 with phased commencement: some rules immediately, the consent-manager rule from 13 November 2026, and most obligations from 13 May 2027. Confirm against the eGazette and any later amendment.
- **EU:** GDPR Chapter V. India has no adequacy decision; use the 2021 Standard Contractual Clauses (Decision 2021/914), binding corporate rules or an Article 49 derogation.
- **UK:** UK GDPR; use the International Data Transfer Agreement or the UK Addendum to the EU SCCs. Check how the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 has changed the transfer test.
- **UAE:** Federal Decree-Law 45 of 2021 (PDPL) is in force but its Executive Regulations had not been issued at the last check. A federal authority for AI and data was announced in June 2026 to take over the data regulator's functions; check who now regulates. The DIFC Data Protection Law No. 5 of 2020 and ADGM Data Protection Regulations 2021 apply in those free zones.
- **US:** no general federal privacy law; check sector laws, State privacy laws, and federal rules restricting transfers of certain bulk sensitive personal data to countries of concern.
